# Chapter 1: Introduction

## Core Idea
Shape Up is Basecamp's product-development method and a toolbox for making meaningful progress. It joins six-week cycles, up-front shaping, deliberate betting, and autonomous teams.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Six-week cycles**: Give a team one fixed period that feels long enough to finish meaningful work and short enough to make the deadline real.
  - When to use: Use a cycle when a team needs a clear end point without daily time control.
  - How: Shape a project first, commit one cycle, protect the team, and aim to ship before the cycle ends.
- **Shaping track and building track**: A small senior group shapes possible future work while cycle teams build work shaped earlier.
  - When to use: Use two tracks when unshaped work would make a committed project risky.
  - How: Solve the main questions before a bet, then hand the team a rough, solved, bounded concept.
- **Appetite**: Ask how much time an idea deserves, rather than estimating how long an unconstrained design might take.
  - When to use: Use appetite before solution design.
  - How: Choose the time budget, then shape a solution that fits it.
- **Targeting risk**: Shape Up targets the risk of not shipping on time, not the separate risk of building the wrong product.
  - When to use: Use this focus when a team cannot act on good product insight.
  - How: Remove rabbit holes before betting, cap the bet, and integrate design and programming early.

## Key Concepts
- **Shaping**: Up-front work that narrows a problem and outlines a solution before a bet.
- **Bet**: A commitment to one project for one cycle with a capped downside.
- **Autonomous team**: A small designer-programmer team that defines tasks and makes implementation trade-offs.
- **Thin-tailed risk**: A project with a small chance of a short delay and no large unknown tail.
- **Virtuous circle**: Better-shaped projects permit more team autonomy, which gives senior people more time to shape better projects.

## Mental Models
- Use a **cycle** as a product-level time box, not as a tool for counting hours.
- Think of **shaping** as designing guard rails, not as writing a complete specification.
- Think of **autonomy** as responsibility for the whole project, not freedom to invent an unbounded project.

## Anti-patterns
- **Execution as everything**: Shipping the wrong way can damage morale, trust, and long-term progress.
- **Unbounded projects**: Long projects hide risk and make teams carry old work.
- **Interrupted commitments**: Pulling people away destroys momentum and weakens the bet.
- **Task-ticket teams**: Assigning disconnected tasks removes ownership of how the parts fit together.

## Worked Example
Basecamp began as an internal tool for a web consultancy. Three people built the first version, while David Heinemeier Hansson worked about ten programming hours each week. The team had to hammer the scope to fit the time available. Later, Basecamp formalized six-week cycles, shaping, and betting as the product team grew. The method then joined a rough solution from senior shapers with a small integrated team that could decide the details during the cycle.

## Key Takeaways
1. Shape the work before asking a team to build it.
2. Set the appetite before designing the solution.
3. Protect a bet with uninterrupted time and a circuit breaker.
4. Integrate design and programming early.
5. Measure success by meaningful work shipped, not by busy hours.
