# Chapter 18: Adjust to Your Size

## Core Idea
Separate Shape Up's basic truths from Basecamp's scale-specific practices. A two- or three-person team may work fluidly, while a larger team needs cycles, cool-down, specialized roles, and a formal betting table.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Basic truths versus specific practices**: Shaping, deliberate betting, capped downside, and known-versus-unknown work apply at every size.
  - When to use: Use this distinction before adapting the method.
  - How: Keep the reason for each practice, then change the ceremony and time scale as needed.
- **Small enough to wing it**: Tiny teams can alternate shaping and building without formal cycles.
  - When to use: Use it when two or three people wear many hats and communicate directly.
  - How: Name the current hat and phase, set an appetite, shape, build, then shape the next thing. Bets may last two or three weeks.
- **Big enough to specialize**: Larger teams need structure because ad-hoc coordination starts to consume time.
  - When to use: Use it when interruptions and communication gaps threaten delivery.
  - How: Add six-week cycles, two-week cool-down, a formal betting table, out-of-cycle shapers, and specialized support roles.

## Key Concepts
- **Basic truth**: A principle that remains valid after a practice changes.
- **Scale-dependent practice**: A process detail that fits a particular team size.
- **Dedicated shaper**: A person who works on future projects outside the build cycle.
- **Specialized role**: A role that protects cycle teams from unrelated work.
- **Fluidity**: The direct communication and flexible phase changes of a tiny team.

## Mental Models
- Think of structure as a **coordination tool**. Add it when communication no longer stays cheap.
- Preserve the **why** of a practice while changing its **how**.
- Treat interruptions as a scale signal. More specialists can protect uninterrupted bets.

## Anti-patterns
- **Rigid small-team process**: Formal cycles and meetings can burden a tiny team that can coordinate directly.
- **Ad-hoc large-team process**: Winging it lets coordination consume time and lets work slip through cracks.
- **Practice copying**: Copying Basecamp's numbers without preserving the basic truths misses the method.

## Worked Example
A team of two or three people may set a two-week appetite, build the work, and switch hats without a formal betting table. At Basecamp's size, about fifty people with roughly a dozen on product, separate shapers, Core Product teams, SIP, Ops, and technical Support roles protect cycle work. The company can then use six-week cycles, cool-down, and a formal table without pulling builders into unrelated work.

## Key Takeaways
1. Keep shaping, deliberate bets, and capped downside at any size.
2. Let tiny teams use lighter structure and variable bet lengths.
3. Add formal cycles when coordination becomes expensive.
4. Specialize roles to protect uninterrupted build time.
5. Adapt practices by reason, not by imitation.
