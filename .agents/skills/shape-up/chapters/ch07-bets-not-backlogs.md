# Chapter 7: Bets, Not Backlogs

## Core Idea
Replace the central backlog with a small set of current, shaped potential bets. Ideas that matter can return with a person, context, and purpose when the timing is right.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **A few potential bets**: The betting table considers pitches shaped during the last six weeks and older pitches someone deliberately revived.
  - When to use: Use this before each cycle.
  - How: Bring only a few risk-reduced pitches. Bet on one, or let it go without carrying it forward.
- **Decentralized lists**: Departments may track requests, bugs, or future ideas in their own systems.
  - When to use: Use local lists when a group needs memory or advocacy.
  - How: Keep these lists independent. They are not direct inputs to the betting table.
- **Important ideas come back**: Repeated customer pain or a truly valuable idea will return to attention.
  - When to use: Use this rule when fear of forgetting creates pressure for a central backlog.
  - How: Let a person bring the idea back with new context at a future table.

## Key Concepts
- **Backlog**: A growing central pile of old tasks that creates review work and false obligation.
- **Potential bet**: A shaped pitch that may receive one cycle of team time.
- **Revived pitch**: An older pitch that someone deliberately brings back for current review.
- **Decentralized list**: A local list owned by a department or person, outside the betting process.

## Mental Models
- Think of the betting table as a **fresh filter**, not a queue.
- Treat memory as **distributed advocacy**. An idea returns because someone still cares and can explain why.
- Use a soft rejection to preserve options without creating a promise.

## Anti-patterns
- **Central backlog**: It makes the company groom old ideas instead of acting on timely work.
- **Automatic carry-over**: Scraps of work enter the next cycle without a new bet.
- **One-time idea worship**: A single mention does not prove that a problem deserves cycle time.

## Worked Example
Support keeps a local list of recurring customer requests. Product keeps its own list of ideas it may shape. In a one-on-one, Support can explain which issue now creates the largest burden. Product may shape one issue for the next betting table. If the table passes, Support can bring it back later with current evidence. No central backlog needs to hold every request.

## Key Takeaways
1. Bring a few shaped pitches to the betting table.
2. Let a passed pitch go unless someone revives it.
3. Keep local lists for memory and advocacy, not central prioritization.
4. Trust important ideas to return with stronger context.
