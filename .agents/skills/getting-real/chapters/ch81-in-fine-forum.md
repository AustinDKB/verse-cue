# Chapter 81: In Fine Forum

## Core Idea
Use forums or web-based group chat so customers can ask questions and help one another. An open customer channel removes the team as the middleman, saves support time, and lets users share the product knowledge they gain from experience.

The team should participate without taking ownership of every exchange. Customers can post tips, tricks, feature requests, stories, and other practical information. The team can enter when assistance is useful, while the community remains the main place for shared help.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Customer-to-Customer Forum — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Let customers answer questions and share experience with other customers.
  - **When to use**: Use it when customers have useful product knowledge and many questions do not need a private team response.
  - **How**: Provide a forum or web-based group chat. Give customers a place to ask, answer, and explain. Let the team add help when the community needs it.
- **Remove the Middleman — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Take the team out of the center of every customer conversation.
  - **When to use**: Use it when the support team repeats answers that customers could share directly.
  - **How**: Create an open stream of communication. Allow customers to exchange solutions and experiences without routing each message through the company.
- **Light-Touch Team Participation — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Support the community without turning the forum into another company-only support queue.
  - **When to use**: Use it when the team needs visibility but the purpose of the forum is peer help.
  - **How**: Visit from time to time. Offer assistance when needed. Let customers keep the main conversation active.

## Key Concepts
- **Forum**: An online place where customers post questions and replies.
- **Web-based group chat**: A shared online conversation for customer discussion.
- **Middleman**: The company role that stands between customers who could help each other.
- **Customer help**: Assistance that one user gives to another user.
- **Tip**: A short piece of practical product advice.
- **Trick**: A useful way to use the product that customers discover.
- **Feature request**: A customer suggestion shared with the wider community.
- **Product story**: A customer account of using the product.
- **Shared experience**: Product knowledge built from customer use.

## Mental Models
- **Community support loop**: One customer’s answer can help many customers without another private support exchange.
- **Open stream**: Public questions and answers remain available for other users to read.
- **Light-touch stewardship**: The team keeps the channel useful without controlling every message.
- **Users as teachers**: People often want to help one another when the product gives them a clear place to do so.

## Anti-patterns
- **Team-only support**: Routing every question through the company wastes time and hides useful customer knowledge.
- **Closed customer conversation**: Private exchanges prevent one answer from helping the next customer.
- **Company monopoly**: Making the team answer every post weakens the community’s role.
- **Absent assistance**: Never entering the forum leaves customers without help when peer knowledge is not enough.
- **Fear of customer help**: Assuming customers will not help one another prevents a useful support channel.

## Worked Example
A product forum lets customers post tips, tricks, feature requests, and stories. A customer who knows a useful method answers another customer’s question. The product team visits from time to time and offers assistance, but customers remain the main source of shared experience. The team saves time because it does not stand between every pair of users.

## Key Takeaways
1. Give customers an open place to ask and answer questions.
2. Let useful answers remain visible to other customers.
3. Visit the forum and assist without owning every exchange.
4. Treat customer experience as a support resource.
5. Use peer help to reduce repeated private support work.
