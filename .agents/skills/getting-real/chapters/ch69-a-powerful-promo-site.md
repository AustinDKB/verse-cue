# Chapter 69: A Powerful Promo Site

## Core Idea
The best promotional tool is a great product that people find useful. A strong promotional site then turns that product into a clear story that helps visitors understand the benefits, see the product, trust the claims, and start using it.

The site should move a visitor from interest to evidence to action. It should show the application, explain its philosophy, provide proof from real customers, and make pricing and signup easy to find. Promotion works best when the site remains useful after launch.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Powerful Promo Site — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Build one promotional home that explains, demonstrates, proves, and sells the application.
  - **When to use**: Use it before launch and keep it active after launch whenever people need a clear path from discovery to signup.
  - **How**: Add an overview of the application and its benefits. Add a guided tour, screen captures, and videos. Explain the product philosophy in a manifesto. Add case studies, customer quotes, reviews, and press. Offer a forum, pricing, signup, and a weblog.
- **Show the Product — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Replace vague promotion with visible evidence of what the application looks like and how people use it.
  - **When to use**: Use it when a visitor cannot understand the product from a short description.
  - **How**: Show real screens and a guided path through important features. Connect each feature to a benefit instead of presenting an isolated list.
## Key Concepts
- **Promotional site**: A public site that explains and presents an application.
- **Overview**: A short explanation of the application and its benefits.
- **Tour**: A guided path through important product features.
- **Screen capture**: A product image that shows the real interface.
- **Manifesto**: A statement of the ideas and philosophy behind the product.
- **Case study**: A real example that shows what customers can achieve.
- **Buzz**: Customer praise, reviews, press, and other public attention.
- **Pricing and signup**: The information and action that move a visitor into the application.
- **Weblog**: A regularly updated source of news, tips, and useful information.

## Mental Models
- **Value before volume**: Treat product usefulness as the first promotional asset, then amplify it with the site.
- **Evidence ladder**: Move visitors from explanation, to demonstration, to outside proof, to signup.
- **One public doorway**: Give every interested person a clear place to learn, evaluate, and enter.
- **Living site**: Treat the promotional site as an ongoing product surface, not a launch-day brochure.

## Anti-patterns
- **Promotion without usefulness**: Loud claims cannot compensate for an application that does not help people.
- **Feature fog**: A list of features without screens, videos, or a tour leaves visitors unable to picture the product.
- **Proof gap**: Claims without case studies, testimonials, reviews, or press give visitors little reason to trust them.
- **Signup maze**: Hidden pricing or a difficult signup path loses people after the site creates interest.
- **Stale brochure**: A site without a weblog or current evidence stops helping after launch.

## Worked Example
Create a public site for a new application. Start with an overview that states the product benefits. Add a tour that walks through the main features, then add screen captures and videos so visitors can see the real experience. Publish a manifesto to explain the ideas behind the product. Add a case study and customer quotes to show results. Give the community a forum where members can help each other. Put pricing and signup within easy reach. Keep the site fresh with a weblog containing news and tips. This sequence reconstructs the source list as one path from understanding to trust to use.

## Key Takeaways
1. Improve product usefulness before adding louder promotion.
2. Show the actual application with a tour, screen captures, and videos.
3. Explain the product philosophy so visitors understand the choices behind it.
4. Use case studies, testimonials, reviews, and press as outside proof.
5. Keep pricing and signup easy to find.
6. Keep the promotional site useful with community help and a weblog.
