# Chapter 43: Get Well Rounded Individuals

## Core Idea
Small teams need quick-learning generalists who can wear different hats. Hire people who can adjust, communicate, and learn as the company changes instead of people locked into one narrow specialty.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Quick-learning generalists — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Prefer broad ability and learning speed over an ingrained specialty.
  - **When to use**: Use it when a small team must cover many needs with few people.
  - **How**: Look for people who can work across design, writing, programming, information organization, and customer communication.
- **Wear different hats — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Treat role changes as normal work on a small team.
  - **When to use**: Use it when the product or company may change direction quickly.
  - **How**: Choose people who shift gears, learn new work, and flow with a new need instead of protecting one fixed task.

## Key Concepts
- **Quick-learning generalist**: A person who learns new work and contributes across several needs.
- **Ingrained specialist**: A person whose narrow skill set limits movement into other work.
- **Broad skill set**: A useful mix of skills that lets a small team cover more work.
- **Information architecture**: Organized thinking about how information fits together.
- **Communication**: The ability to explain work and speak with customers.
- **Role shift**: A move from one kind of work to another as the team changes.
- **Stick-in-the-mud**: A person who can do only one thing and resists change.

## Mental Models
- **Small-team coverage**: Each person should cover more than one need because the team has few people.
- **Learning speed over labels**: Ask how fast a person can learn instead of trusting a narrow job title.
- **Gear-shift test**: Picture a changed direction and ask whether the candidate can move with it.

## Anti-patterns
- **Narrow-title hiring**: A precise title hides gaps that a small team cannot afford.
- **Role-wall thinking**: Fixed boundaries stop people from helping with nearby work.
- **Specialist comfort**: A person who performs one task well may fail when the company changes direction.
- **Skill-count hiring**: Counting one deep skill without checking learning and communication creates a fragile team.

## Worked Example
A small team could reject an information architect as an overly narrow hire. It could choose a person who can organize information, write, communicate with customers, and help with design or programming. If the product changes direction, that person can shift gears rather than wait for a new specialist.

## Key Takeaways
1. Hire for learning speed and broad ability.
2. Test whether the person can wear different hats.
3. Value communication with customers.
4. Reject narrow roles that leave nearby work uncovered.
5. Check whether the person can shift when the company changes direction.
