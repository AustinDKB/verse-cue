# Chapter 40: Hire Less and Hire Later

## Core Idea
More people do not automatically create faster work. Delay hiring until the team proves that the work matters, cannot disappear through a practice change or small software solution, and needs a specific person to relieve a specific pain.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Add slow to go fast — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Grow carefully so the team can preserve culture, direction, and communication.
  - **When to use**: Use it when a company has access to many skilled people, even 100 excellent people, or feels pressure to grow early.
  - **How**: Do not hire everyone at once. First ask if the work needs to exist, if a practice can remove it, or if a slice of software can solve it. Hire only when no simpler answer works.
- **Need-before-hire test — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Prove the need for a role before opening it.
  - **When to use**: Use it when a person’s absence creates a burden but the burden may not justify a new role.
  - **How**: Test how long the team can work without the position. If a hire remains necessary, define the person, the introduction to the work, and the exact pain the person should relieve.
- **Brooks’s Law — Fred Brooks**: Adding people to a late software project makes it later.
  - **When to use**: Use it when a late project seems to need more people immediately.
  - **How**: Count the training and communication cost before adding staff. New people need context, and current people must spend time providing it.
- **Quality over headcount — Joel Spolsky**: Several mediocre programmers do not equal a small number of excellent programmers.
  - **When to use**: Use it when a team proposes more people as a substitute for skill or clear ownership.
  - **How**: Prefer strong individual work on clear tasks. Five Antonio Salieris will not produce Mozart’s *Requiem*. Avoid coordination overhead that prevents a larger group from producing coherent results.

## Key Concepts
- **Slow growth**: Deliberate hiring that protects team coherence.
- **Hiring pressure**: The belief that every burden needs another person.
- **Necessary work**: Work that the product or business must actually keep.
- **Practice change**: A new way of working that removes a burden.
- **Software slice**: A narrow software solution that replaces unnecessary manual work.
- **Training headache**: Time current workers spend teaching new people.
- **Communication lapse**: Lost information caused by a larger, less connected team.
- **Coordination overhead**: Work required to keep several people aligned.
- **Specific pain**: The exact burden that a new hire must relieve.

## Mental Models
- **Absence test**: Before replacing a role, observe how long the team can operate without it.
- **Work-removal ladder**: First remove the work, then change the practice, then use a small software solution, and hire last.
- **Headcount tax**: Each new person adds training, communication, and direction cost.
- **Few strong hands**: A small group of capable people can outperform a larger group with weaker skills.

## Anti-patterns
- **Early hiring spree**: A large intake overwhelms culture and creates training, personality, and communication problems.
- **Replacement reflex**: Fill a vacant position before learning whether the work still needs it.
- **Late-project staffing rush**: New people add context and coordination work to an already late project.
- **Headcount as cure**: Add people instead of removing unnecessary work or changing the practice.
- **Mediocrity by volume**: Use many weak contributors and expect their total effort to equal strong execution.

## Worked Example
Jack Welch used an absence test after firing someone. He did not replace the person immediately, and he watched how long the company could operate without the position. This example supports delaying a replacement until the need becomes clear. The chapter does not recommend firing people for this test. Apply the same question to any open role: can the team remove the work, change the practice, or use a small software slice before it hires?

## Key Takeaways
1. Delay hiring until the need becomes clear.
2. Remove unnecessary work before assigning it to a new person.
3. Test a practice change or small software solution first.
4. Define the exact pain a hire must relieve.
5. Count training and communication cost before adding staff.
6. Prefer strong individual contributors over headcount for its own sake.
