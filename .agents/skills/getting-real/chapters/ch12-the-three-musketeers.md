# Chapter 12: The Three Musketeers

## Core Idea
Start version 1.0 with three people: a developer, a designer, and a sweeper who can move between both worlds. This team size gives enough manpower while keeping communication and tradeoffs manageable.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Three Musketeers — Basecamp**: Use a three-person team for the first version of an app.
  - **When to use**: Use it for version 1.0, when the product still needs discovery and the team must stay streamlined and agile.
  - **How**: Assign development, design, and sweeping work across a developer, a designer, and a sweeper. Talented people can thrive within restraints. If three people cannot build version one, change the team or reduce the initial version.
- **Metcalfe’s Law corollary — Marc Hedlund**: Metcalfe’s Law states that the value of a communication system grows at approximately the square of its number of users. Hedlund applies the opposite pattern to project teams: team efficiency is approximately the inverse of the square of the number of members.
  - **When to use**: Use this rule when deciding whether a new team member will add capacity or add communication cost.
  - **How**: Start with fewer people than the plan suggests, then reduce the number again. Add a person only when the work needs that person more than the team needs simple communication.
- **Communication flow — Steve McConnell**: Communication paths rise multiplicatively, in proportion to the square of the number of people.
  - **When to use**: Use this model before increasing a project team.
  - **How**: Count the relationships that need to stay informed. Keep the team small so decisions and customer communication do not require many paths.

## Key Concepts
- **Version 1.0**: The first small, usable version of the app.
- **Developer**: The team member responsible for programming work.
- **Designer**: The team member responsible for product and interface design.
- **Sweeper**: A person who can move between development and design.
- **Manpower**: The people and work capacity available to build the product.
- **Tradeoff**: A choice that gives up one option to protect another.
- **Communication path**: A relationship through which information must travel.
- **Team efficiency**: The team’s ability to work without communication cost overwhelming output.
- **Small team**: A team with few members and a short communication flow.

## Mental Models
- **Three roles, one product**: Cover development, design, and the space between them without building separate departments.
- **People add paths**: Each new person adds more relationships that need communication.
- **Short loop, early tradeoffs**: A small team faces priorities sooner and can decide without leaving people out.
- **Team size tests scope**: If three capable people cannot build version one, the version may be too large or the team may be wrong.

## Anti-patterns
- **Starting with a large team**: More people create more communication paths and reduce efficiency.
- **Adding people to solve every problem**: More manpower can increase coordination work instead of product output.
- **Oversized version one**: A broad first release delays the test of whether the idea has wings.
- **Leaving people out of the loop**: A growing communication network makes shared understanding harder.
- **Keeping the wrong team**: If three people cannot build the first version, refuse neither a team change nor a scope reduction.

## Worked Example
Plan a first release with three people: one developer, one designer, and one sweeper. Keep the first version small and tight so the team can make tradeoffs early and communicate directly. If the team cannot build that version, do not solve the problem by adding people automatically. Choose different people or slim down the release. Then test whether the idea has wings before expanding the base.

## Key Takeaways
1. Start version 1.0 with a developer, designer, and sweeper.
2. Reduce the first release before increasing the team.
3. Treat communication paths as a cost of team growth.
4. Make tradeoffs early while the team can still communicate directly.
5. Add people only when their value exceeds the communication cost.
6. Use the first small release to test whether the idea has wings.
