# Chapter 71: Solicit Early

## Core Idea
Start collecting launch interest as soon as the product idea has a name and a small public signal. The first public site does not need a finished application. A domain name, a logo, and one or two sentences that describe or hint at the product can create a foundation of people who want launch news.

Early solicitation changes an empty launch into a launch with an audience. It also creates a direct permission path. People who give an email address ask to hear from the team, so the team can contact them when the application becomes ready.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Solicit Early — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Ask for launch interest before the application reaches release quality.
  - **When to use**: Use it as soon as the team has selected a domain name and can share a basic product direction.
  - **How**: Put up the domain, add a logo, and write one or two sentences that describe or hint at the application. Add an email field. Save the addresses for launch news.
- **Advance Buzz and Signups — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Turn early curiosity into a waiting audience.
  - **When to use**: Use it during the period before a teaser, preview, or public launch.
  - **How**: Give people a small but clear reason to care. Let them choose to receive notice. Keep the page simple so the signup action remains easy.

## Key Concepts
- **Early solicitation**: Asking for product interest before launch.
- **Launch interest**: A person’s request to hear about the application.
- **Foundation**: The initial group that supports a later launch.
- **Domain name**: The public web address selected for the product.
- **Logo**: A simple visual identity for the early product page.
- **Product hint**: A short description that reveals direction without requiring a finished application.
- **Email address**: The contact path used to notify interested people.
- **Waiting audience**: People ready to receive a launch notice.

## Mental Models
- **Empty room to waiting room**: A simple interest page gives an unfinished product a public audience before release.
- **Small signal**: A logo and a short hint can test whether the product idea creates curiosity.
- **Permission path**: An email signup changes unknown attention into consent to receive launch news.
- **Interest foundation**: Each early signup makes the later launch less dependent on last-minute promotion.

## Anti-patterns
- **Polish delay**: Waiting for a finished site wastes the time when curiosity could start building.
- **Silent build**: Keeping the product private until launch leaves no audience ready for the announcement.
- **Information overload**: Explaining every unfinished detail can create confusion instead of interest.
- **Interest without capture**: Receiving attention without asking for an email loses the clearest path to later contact.
- **Signup friction**: A complex early page makes a low-commitment action feel too costly.

## Worked Example
Choose the product domain and place a logo on a simple page. Add one or two sentences that describe, or at least hint at, what the application will do. Add an email field and let visitors request launch notice. Keep the page focused on the idea rather than unfinished details. At launch, use the saved addresses to tell the waiting audience that the application is ready. This reconstruction follows the source’s minimum sequence without requiring a finished promotional site.

## Key Takeaways
1. Put up an interest page as soon as the product has a name and direction.
2. Use a logo and one or two sentences instead of waiting for a finished site.
3. Ask visitors for permission to send launch news.
4. Keep the early page simple and easy to understand.
5. Treat the email list as the foundation of the later launch audience.
