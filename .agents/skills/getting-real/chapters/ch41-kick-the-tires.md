# Chapter 41: Kick the Tires

## Core Idea
A portfolio, resume, code sample, or previous project shows past work, not how a person works with the current team. Give a prospective employee a small paid project and observe the work, communication, and fit before making a full hire.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Kick the tires — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Test a prospective employee through real work before hiring.
  - **When to use**: Use it when documents and examples cannot show how the person will work with the team.
  - **How**: Give the person a small project. Observe how the person designs or codes, communicates, handles the work, and fits the team’s working style.
- **Test drive — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Treat a short working period as a mutual evaluation.
  - **When to use**: Use it when a full hire would create substantial risk for either side.
  - **How**: Work together for about 20 or 40 hours when possible. Make the result and the working relationship clear enough for both sides to judge.
- **Start small — Suzanne Falter-Barns**: Begin a virtual assistant relationship with one or two test assignments.
  - **When to use**: Use it when early optimism may hide work or chemistry problems.
  - **How**: State that the assignment is a test run. Review the result and the relationship before giving the person all of the work.

## Key Concepts
- **Test drive**: A short work period before a hiring decision.
- **Small project**: A limited assignment that reveals working behavior.
- **Communication**: The way a person exchanges information during work.
- **Working fit**: Compatibility between a person and the team’s way of working.
- **Chemistry**: The practical relationship that develops through shared work.
- **Test run**: A clearly stated trial before a larger commitment.
- **Hiring risk**: Trouble and cost caused by a poor fit.
- **Portfolio evidence**: Past work that cannot show current collaboration.

## Mental Models
- **Work beats claims**: Direct observation gives stronger evidence than a description of past work.
- **Small commitment**: Reduce risk by limiting the first assignment.
- **Mutual test**: Let both the team and the candidate judge the fit before commitment.

## Anti-patterns
- **Portfolio-only hiring**: Past work does not show current communication or working fit.
- **Full-work leap**: Giving all work at once hides problems until the cost becomes high.
- **Rose-colored evaluation**: Early optimism makes the team ignore warning signs.
- **Unclear trial**: A test without a stated trial boundary creates confusion about the commitment.

## Worked Example
Before hiring, Basecamp gives a prospective team member a small project. The team may watch the person design or code a few screens and work together for 20 or 40 hours. It observes the person’s work, communication, and fit rather than relying only on a portfolio or resume. The team states that the assignment tests the relationship. If the fit is good or bad, the evidence becomes clear before either side accepts the larger risk.

## Key Takeaways
1. Use a small real assignment before a full hire.
2. Observe work and communication together.
3. State that the assignment is a test run.
4. Limit the first commitment to reduce risk.
5. Let both sides judge the working fit.
