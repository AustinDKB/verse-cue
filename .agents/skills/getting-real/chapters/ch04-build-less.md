# Chapter 4: Build Less

## Core Idea
Do less than competitors to beat them. Avoid the expensive and defensive race to add more features, spend more money, and build a larger organization.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Underdo your competition**: Use this rule when a competitor appears to lead through feature count or spending. One-down instead of one-up. Solve simple problems well and leave the hairy, difficult, nasty problems to others.
- **Less as a competitive strategy**: Apply reduction to features, options, preferences, people, corporate structure, meetings, abstractions, and promises. Use less to stay able to think ahead instead of following competitors.

## Key Concepts
- **One-upping**: Adding more than a competitor as a defensive response.
- **One-downing**: Choosing less than a competitor on purpose.
- **Underdoing**: Building a simpler product instead of matching every competing feature.
- **Cold War mentality**: The expensive, defensive, paranoid race to exceed the other side.
- **Simple problems**: Problems a focused product can solve well.
- **Hairy problems**: Difficult problems that the chapter advises leaving to others.

## Mental Models
- **Less can lead**: A product does not need more features to win.
- **Follow or think ahead**: Defensive companies look behind at competitors, while leaner companies can think ahead.
- **Scope as a choice**: Select a small problem and solve it instead of accepting every difficult problem as required scope.

## Anti-patterns
- **Feature arms race**: If a competitor has four features, adding five, fifteen, or twenty-five creates unnecessary cost.
- **Spending arms race**: Matching a competitor's spending with more spending creates defensive waste.
- **Paranoid following**: Building a company that reacts to competitors prevents leadership.
- **Excess structure**: More people, meetings, abstractions, and promises add weight without proving value.

## Worked Example
If a competitor has four features, do not automatically add a fifth. Choose a simple problem, solve it well, and leave the difficult problems to the competitor. This is one-downing: the product wins through focus rather than through a larger feature list.

## Key Takeaways
1. Underdo competitors instead of copying their excess.
2. Solve simple problems and leave difficult problems outside the product's scope.
3. Reduce features, options, people, meetings, abstractions, and promises.
4. Avoid defensive thinking because it makes the company follow.
