# Chapter 42: Actions, Not Words

## Core Idea
Judge technical candidates by sustained work instead of degrees, resumes, references, or claims. Open source provides visible evidence of work quality, cultural choices, passion, completion, and social fit.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Actions, Not Words — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Replace weak hiring signals with evidence from a candidate’s public work.
  - **When to use**: Use it when you hire programmers and need to judge more than credentials or interview answers.
  - **How**: Review releases, code, tests, design choices, community arguments, completion patterns, and conduct over time.
- **Open Source Passion — Jarkko Laine**: A trace of commitment in open source shows passion for programming.
  - **When to use**: Use it when you need evidence that a candidate cares about the craft outside a hiring conversation.
  - **How**: Check the candidate’s length and depth of participation, not only the presence of one public project.

## Key Concepts
- **Open-source contribution**: Public work that lets a team inspect a programmer’s actions over time.
- **Quality of work**: The actual skill shown in code, tests, releases, and programming practice.
- **Cultural perspective**: The values and ideals that guide technical and community decisions.
- **Level of passion**: The care shown through voluntary, sustained programming work.
- **Completion percentage**: The share of started work that reaches a usable release.
- **Pragmatic trade-off**: A deliberate compromise that helps a person ship useful software.
- **Social match**: The fit shown through manners, communication, and behavior during stress and calm.
- **Secondary factor**: A weak hiring signal that matters less than the quality of a candidate’s work.

## Mental Models
- **Actions over claims**: Treat a candidate’s work as stronger evidence than a resume or reference.
- **Five-signal review**: Inspect quality, cultural perspective, passion, completion, and social match together.
- **Trace of commitment**: Read sustained open-source participation as evidence of care for the craft.
- **Ship-or-stall test**: Ask whether the candidate finishes work and accepts the trade-offs required to release it.

## Anti-patterns
- **Credential-first hiring**: A degree or GPA does not show how a programmer writes, tests, finishes, or works with others.
- **Resume trust**: A resume and reference depend on claims that the hiring team cannot inspect in detail.
- **Talk-only evaluation**: A candidate can describe programming well and still fail to produce good software.
- **Culture-blind review**: Ignoring decisions in code and community work creates conflict after hiring.
- **Extracurricular suspicion**: Assuming open-source work reduces job focus ignores the link between passion and productive work.

## Worked Example
Basecamp followed Jamis’s releases and participation in the Ruby community before hiring him. The team could judge his work quality, cultural fit, passion, completion behavior, and social conduct from visible actions. It did not need to rely on secondary factors. The same evidence also showed that strong open-source contributors could help drive Basecamp technically while continuing their Rails work.

## Key Takeaways
1. Review public technical work before trusting hiring claims.
2. Judge quality through code, tests, releases, and practice.
3. Look for cultural fit in technical and community decisions.
4. Check whether the candidate finishes and ships work.
5. Treat passion as visible commitment, not interview energy.
6. Filter for social fit during both calm and stressful work.
