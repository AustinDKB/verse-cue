# Chapter 9: It Shouldn't be a Chore

## Core Idea
Build a product that you care about and can enjoy making. Keep the work small and manageable so passion can appear in the result and indifference cannot hide behind polish.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Passion as a product signal — Basecamp**: Use this test when choosing a product or reviewing its direction. If the app does not excite the team, the problem may sit in the idea, the scope, or the motive.
  - **When to use**: Use it before a long build and whenever the work feels like a burden.
  - **How**: Keep the app small enough to enjoy. Ask whether the team would use and improve it without a quick cash-out goal. Treat sustained interest as evidence that the product deserves more work.
- **The presence of passion — Khoi Vinh**: Use this design model when polished work still feels cold or empty. Passion and indifference both show through the emotional investment of the people who built the product.
  - **When to use**: Use it during design review and product quality review.
  - **How**: Look beyond attractive presentation. Check whether the work shows care, attention, and a real commitment to the problem.
- **The bakery model — Ian MacKaye**: Use this business model when deciding whether growth or exit should define the company.
  - **When to use**: Use it when a team treats the product as something to sell and leave.
  - **How**: Enjoy making the product, sell good work, serve people who like it, sell more, and keep the bakery operating because the work and customer response matter.

## Key Concepts
- **Chore**: Work that feels burdensome instead of engaging.
- **Passion**: Genuine care for the product and the work.
- **Indifference**: Lack of care that users can still detect.
- **Emotional investment**: The human care visible in a product’s design.
- **Polish**: Surface quality that cannot conceal missing commitment.
- **Cash-out motive**: A focus on a quick financial exit.
- **Bakery model**: Continue making good work because people value it.
- **Manageable scope**: A product size that permits enjoyable, sustained work.

## Mental Models
- **The product shows its maker**: Treat visible care or indifference as part of product quality.
- **Enjoyment supports endurance**: Use work that can sustain attention over the years needed to improve it.
- **Scope protects passion**: Reduce the product until the team can stay engaged with the work.
- **Bakery over extraction**: Prefer a durable practice of making and serving over a plan to absorb value and leave.

## Anti-patterns
- **Building only to cash out**: The motive can show in the product and leaves little reason to improve it.
- **Making the app a chore**: Large or dull work drains the care that gives the product character.
- **Hiding indifference under polish**: Attractive design cannot conceal a lack of emotional investment.
- **Treating customers as an exit path**: A sell-and-leave model replaces good work and happy users with extraction.

## Worked Example
Ian MacKaye described the bakery model: enjoy baking, sell the bread, let people like it, sell more, and keep the bakery operating because it makes good food and people are happy. Applied to software, the team keeps improving a product it cares about instead of building only to sell the company.

## Key Takeaways
1. Choose work that you can care about for a long time.
2. Keep the product small enough to enjoy building.
3. Review for visible care, not only surface polish.
4. Treat indifference as a product problem.
5. Build a durable business around good work and customer value.
