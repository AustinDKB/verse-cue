# Chapter 65: Easy On, Easy Off

## Core Idea
Make signup and cancellation painless. People should enter the product with little effort, try it without a credit card, leave without intimidation, and take their data with them.

Easy exit builds trust. A customer who stays should stay because the product continues to earn the relationship, not because the company hides a cancellation link or holds the customer’s information hostage.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Easy On, Easy Off — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Make both entry and exit simple, clear, and voluntary.
  - **When to use**: Use it whenever the product has signup, trial, billing, cancellation, or data export flows.
  - **How**: Put a clear signup button on the marketing site. Keep the form short. Offer a free option without a credit card. Make cancellation a clear account link and provide data export.
- **Painless Trial — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Let anyone try the product without a call, appointment, special password, or payment barrier.
  - **When to use**: Use it when a potential customer needs direct experience before choosing a plan.
  - **How**: Offer free access at any time. Tell people how fast they can move from signup to login. Ask only for information that the trial needs.
- **Data Bridge — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman, with Charlie O’Donnell, analyst at Union Square Ventures, from “10 Steps to a Hugely Successful Web 2.0 Company”**: Let a departing customer carry created content to another service.
  - **When to use**: Use it when customers create messages, comments, files, or other information inside the product.
  - **How**: Provide a clear export path and a useful format. Keep the customer in control of the information after departure.

## Key Concepts
- **Signup**: The short process that starts product use.
- **Free option**: A trial path that does not require payment.
- **Credit-card-free trial**: Free access without collecting card information first.
- **Cancellation**: A clear action that stops the account without a trap.
- **Data export**: A way to retrieve created information from the product.
- **XML format**: The export format used by Basecamp for messages and comments.
- **Data island**: Information held inside one service until the owner creates a bridge out.
- **Customer control**: The customer’s ability to decide how to use and move information.
- **Trust**: Confidence created when the product respects choice and ownership.

## Mental Models
- **Two-way door**: Treat signup and cancellation as equally valid directions through the product.
- **Data bridge**: Give customers a path from the product’s information island to another place.
- **Voluntary retention**: Keep customers by feeding them value, not by trapping them.
- **Trust through control**: Make control of information visible because choice strengthens the relationship.

## Anti-patterns
- **Gatekept trial**: A callback, appointment, password, or card requirement delays a person who wants to learn.
- **Signup burden**: A long form asks for information that the product does not need.
- **Cancellation trap**: A hidden link, email request, special form, or forced questions makes departure intimidating.
- **Data hostage**: The product keeps customer-created information difficult to export.
- **Penalty exit**: The company uses friction or cost to keep someone who no longer wants the service.

## Worked Example
Basecamp put a clear “Cancel my account” link on the account page. The customer did not need to send email, fill out a special form, or answer questions. Unlike competitors requiring a callback, appointment, or special password, anyone could try free at any time. Basecamp also let customers export all messages and comments in XML. The customer could try, use, leave, and retain control of created information.

## Key Takeaways
1. Put a clear signup action on every useful marketing page.
2. Keep signup short and ask only for needed information.
3. Offer a free trial without a credit card.
4. Make cancellation visible and free of intimidation.
5. Export customer-created data in a useful format.
6. Keep customers through value instead of lock-in.
