# Chapter 90: Go With the Flow

## Core Idea
A web app can change direction while the team learns. Do not treat the original idea as permanent. Watch how the product develops, admit when another path looks more plausible, and adjust toward the stronger opportunity.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Go With the Flow — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Stay open to new paths and changes in direction.
  - **When to use**: Use it when work on the product shows that the original idea does not offer the strongest opportunity.
  - **How**: Use the fluidity of a web app. Tweak and change as you go. Admit mistakes, compare the available paths, and follow the path that appears more plausible.
- **The Surfer Model — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Watch where the large waves break and adjust the product toward them.
  - **When to use**: Use it when customer response or product use reveals a stronger direction than the initial plan.
  - **How**: Watch the changing conditions. Find the big wave. Adjust rather than remain fixed on the starting position.
- **Plausible Path Selection — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Choose the product path that has stronger evidence, even when it differs from the first idea.
  - **When to use**: Use it when the team must decide whether to continue a weak original concept or develop a more promising part.
  - **How**: Compare the paths by plausibility. Shelve the weaker path when the stronger one can become the product.

## Key Concepts
- **Fluidity**: The ability of a web app to change without waiting years for another release.
- **Original idea**: The first product direction, which may not remain the best direction.
- **Change of direction**: A deliberate move from the first path toward a stronger one.
- **Plausible product**: The path that appears more likely to become a useful product.
- **Mistake admission**: Recognition that the first plan does not deserve continued work.
- **Big wave**: A strong opportunity that appears through changing product or customer conditions.
- **Adjustment**: A change that aligns the product with the stronger opportunity.

## Mental Models
- **The product as a moving vessel**: Keep the web app fluid so the team can steer while learning.
- **The surfer watches conditions**: Look for the strongest wave instead of defending the wave first chosen.
- **Evidence outranks attachment**: Let the more plausible path replace the original idea when the work supports it.

## Anti-patterns
- **Original-idea rigidity**: Treating the first plan as fixed prevents the team from following a stronger product path.
- **Sunk-cost attachment**: Continuing a weak direction because the team already invested in it wastes the fluidity of a web app.
- **Mistake denial**: Refusing to admit that the original idea is weaker delays the better product.
- **Passive drift**: Letting the product change without choosing a direction fails to use the evidence from the new path.

## Worked Example
Flickr began as a multiplayer online game called The Game Neverending. Its creators saw that photo sharing was a more plausible product than the game. They eventually shelved the game and followed the photo-sharing path. The example shows how a team can admit that the original idea is not the best one and steer toward the larger wave.

## Key Takeaways
1. Treat a web app as fluid instead of fixed until the next major release.
2. Watch product use for evidence of a stronger path.
3. Admit mistakes when the original idea loses plausibility.
4. Follow the larger opportunity instead of protecting the first plan.
5. Adjust deliberately after you identify the stronger direction.
