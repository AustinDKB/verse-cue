# Chapter 33: Done!

## Core Idea
“Done” marks a completed decision, not a perfect decision. Make a quick, simple call, move forward, and change it later when real use shows that it fails.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Done as momentum — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Use “done” to close a decision and preserve forward movement.
  - **When to use**: Use it when analysis delays a product choice, feature, or implementation step.
  - **How**: Make the smallest sound decision, mark the work done, and move to the next task. Revisit the choice only when evidence shows a problem.
- **Temporary decisions — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Treat product decisions as revisable while the team learns.
  - **When to use**: Use it when a team fears a wrong call and seeks certainty before acting.
  - **How**: Accept that mistakes will occur, correct them quickly, and avoid paralysis through analysis. The low cost of changing a web application makes movement more valuable than perfect prediction.
- **Be an Executioner and execution multiplier — Derek Sivers**: Ideas have little value until execution turns them into results.
  - **When to use**: Use it when a team protects an idea instead of showing working results.
  - **How**: Judge ideas through delivery. Multiply the idea value by the execution value, because a brilliant idea without execution produces little value.

## Key Concepts
- **Done**: A decision or task that reaches a usable stopping point.
- **Momentum**: Forward movement created by repeated completed decisions.
- **Temporary decision**: A choice that can change after evidence arrives.
- **Paralysis through analysis**: Delay caused by seeking certainty before action.
- **Correction speed**: How quickly a team can repair a wrong choice.
- **Execution**: The work that turns an idea into a result.
- **Execution multiplier**: The product of idea value and execution value.
- **Mistake**: A wrong call that matters less when the team corrects it quickly.

## Mental Models
- **Done as a gate**: Close the current decision before opening the next one.
- **Decisions as drafts**: Treat each choice as useful now, not permanent forever.
- **Execution over protection**: Show the result instead of guarding an untested idea.
- **Speed of correction**: Prefer a reversible quick call over a delayed attempt at certainty.

## Anti-patterns
- **Analysis paralysis**: Endless thought delays action, drains morale, and prevents learning from use.
- **Decision protection**: Treat a small product choice as irreversible, even though a web application can change.
- **Idea hoarding**: Protect an idea or demand secrecy before anyone sees execution. An unexecuted idea has little practical value.
- **Perfection before movement**: Wait for a flawless answer and lose the momentum that completed work creates.

## Worked Example
Derek Sivers assigns values to ideas and execution. An awful idea has value `-1`, a weak idea `1`, a so-so idea `5`, a good idea `10`, a great idea `15`, and a brilliant idea `20`. No execution has value `$1`, weak execution `$1,000`, so-so execution `$10,000`, good execution `$100,000`, great execution `$1,000,000`, and brilliant execution `$10,000,000`. Multiply the two values. A brilliant idea with no execution equals `$20`. A brilliant idea with great execution equals `$20,000,000`. The example makes execution the practical multiplier.

## Key Takeaways
1. Mark a sound, reversible decision “done” and continue.
2. Accept temporary decisions instead of seeking perfect certainty.
3. Correct wrong calls quickly when real use provides evidence.
4. Measure ideas by execution, not by how carefully people protect them.
5. Build momentum through a steady rhythm of completed work.
