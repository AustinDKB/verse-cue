# Chapter 67: A Softer Bullet

## Core Idea
Deliver bad news with advance notice and a grandfather period. A price increase or similar change should give customers time to understand the change and should show existing customers that the company values them.

Existing customers provide the product’s base of support. Treating them as if they were new prospects can make a necessary change feel like a gouge. A softer change protects trust while still allowing the company to change its terms.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Softer Bullet — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Reduce the impact of bad news through time and special protection.
  - **When to use**: Use it before a price increase or another change that makes the customer’s current arrangement worse.
  - **How**: Give plenty of advance notice. Consider a grandfather period that exempts existing customers for a stated time.
- **Value Existing Customers — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Treat current customers as the product’s bread and butter.
  - **When to use**: Use it when a business must change pricing or terms for people who already support it.
  - **How**: Tell them early, protect them for a period, and avoid making them feel gouged or forgotten.

## Key Concepts
- **Advance notice**: Information given early enough for a customer to prepare.
- **Grandfather period**: A time when existing customers keep older terms.
- **Price increase**: A change that raises the customer’s payment.
- **Existing customer**: A person who already uses and supports the product.
- **Customer value**: The importance of treating current support as something to respect.
- **Trust**: Confidence preserved when a business handles bad news openly and fairly.

## Mental Models
- **Impact buffer**: Time between notice and change gives people room to respond.
- **Trust account**: A grandfather period adds goodwill when the company must ask for more.
- **Fairness signal**: Special care for existing customers shows that loyalty matters.

## Anti-patterns
- **Surprise increase**: Immediate bad news leaves customers no time to plan.
- **Universal shock**: Applying a new price to everyone at once ignores the customer relationship.
- **Gouging signal**: Removing all protection makes existing customers feel punished for support.
- **Silent change**: Hiding the change damages trust more than the change itself.

## Worked Example
A company needs to increase its price. It tells customers well before the new price starts. It gives existing customers a grandfather period that keeps their current terms for a time. The company still changes its pricing, but customers receive preparation time and a clear sign that their support matters.

## Key Takeaways
1. Give customers plenty of notice before bad news takes effect.
2. Use a grandfather period when a price increase affects existing customers.
3. Treat current customers as the product’s bread and butter.
4. Protect trust by avoiding surprise and gouging.
