# Chapter 88: Keep Up With the Joneses

## Core Idea
Stay informed about both your product and your competitors. Subscribe to news feeds that track company names and product names. RSS can deliver changing information to you, so you remain up to speed without waiting for a major announcement.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Product-and-Competitor Feed Watch — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Monitor news about your own product and the products that compete with it.
  - **When to use**: Use it when the product operates in a changing market and the team needs regular awareness.
  - **How**: Subscribe to feeds for your company name, product name, competitor names, and competitor product names. Read the incoming items as an ongoing information stream.
- **RSS as an Always-Up-to-Date Channel — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Let RSS deliver changing information instead of checking each source separately.
  - **When to use**: Use it when relevant news changes often and manual searches can miss updates.
  - **How**: Put the selected keyword feeds into an RSS reader or another feed service. Use the delivered information to stay current.
- **Know the Enemy — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Treat competitor knowledge as a normal part of product awareness.
  - **When to use**: Use it when the team focuses only on its own work and loses sight of other products.
  - **How**: Track competitors with the same care as your own product. Learn what information reaches the market and keep the team up to speed.

## Key Concepts
- **News feed**: A channel that delivers new items about selected subjects.
- **RSS**: A feed format that sends changing information to a reader or service.
- **Keyword**: A company name or product name used to find relevant items.
- **Product monitoring**: Ongoing attention to news about your own product.
- **Competitor monitoring**: Ongoing attention to news about competing companies and products.
- **Up to speed**: Current enough to understand recent changes and information.
- **Feed service**: A tool that collects and delivers selected news feeds.

## Mental Models
- **The market as a live stream**: Product information changes continuously, so use a channel that also updates continuously.
- **Own product and enemy together**: Product awareness is incomplete when competitor information is missing.
- **Keywords as listening points**: Company and product names provide focused points for market listening.

## Anti-patterns
- **Own-product tunnel vision**: Watching only your product hides changes from competitors and weakens market awareness.
- **Manual-only checking**: Visiting sources one at a time makes ongoing information harder to track.
- **No keyword plan**: Subscribing without company and product names makes the feed less useful for the intended watch.
- **Stale awareness**: Relying on old knowledge leaves the team out of date while the market changes.

## Worked Example
A product team creates feed subscriptions for its company name, its product name, each competitor name, and each competing product name. PubSub, Technorati, Feedster, or another service delivers matching items through RSS. The team can then follow changing product and competitor news in one ongoing stream.

## Key Takeaways
1. Subscribe to feeds about your product and your competitors.
2. Use company names and product names as keywords.
3. Use RSS to receive changing information as it arrives.
4. Treat competitor awareness as part of normal product work.
