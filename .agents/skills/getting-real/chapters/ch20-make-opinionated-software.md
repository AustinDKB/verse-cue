# Chapter 20: Make Opinionated Software

## Core Idea
A strong application takes sides. Users seek an approach and a vision, not an endless list of flexible features. Define the product's position, build for people who share it, and accept that other people can choose another product.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Opinionated software — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Use a clear product position instead of trying to remain agnostic.
  - **When to use**: Use it when feature requests or pressure for flexibility begin to weaken the product's purpose.
  - **How**: Decide what the product believes, choose the features that express that belief, and reject work that serves people the product cannot make happy. Treat compatible customers as partners.
- **Ego-less and time-less content — Ward Cunningham and friends**: Use this collaboration model when ownership and edit time distract from shared content.
  - **When to use**: Use it when a product must support a community rather than emphasize individual credit.
  - **How**: Remove visual signals that the team considers unimportant. The original wiki removed strong representation of who wrote a change and when it occurred, which made the content more communal.
- **On the bus or off the bus — Basecamp team**: Use this decision rule when a product's vision excludes some preferences.
  - **When to use**: Use it when the team must choose between protecting the product's approach and chasing users who want another approach.
  - **How**: Keep the position clear. Invite people who share it, and let people who reject it choose another product.

## Key Concepts
- **Opinionated software**: Software that expresses a clear approach and product vision.
- **Agnostic software**: Software that avoids a position by trying to support every approach.
- **Product vision**: The belief that determines what the product includes and leaves out.
- **Approach**: The way a product expects people to solve a problem.
- **Feature flexibility**: The ability to support many different preferences or workflows.
- **Ego-less content**: Content that does not emphasize individual ownership.
- **Time-less content**: Content that does not emphasize when each change occurred.
- **Product partner**: A customer who shares the product's vision and approach.

## Mental Models
- **Software as a point of view**: Treat product choices as statements about the best way to solve the problem.
- **Limits create identity**: What the product leaves out helps people understand what it is.
- **Partner over audience**: Seek customers who share the product's position instead of trying to satisfy everyone.
- **Remove distracting ownership**: Hide information that does not support the community's main work.

## Anti-patterns
- **Agnostic by default**: Endless flexibility removes the direction users came to find.
- **Chasing incompatible customers**: Work spent on people who reject the vision makes the product less useful to its intended partners.
- **Feature accumulation**: Adding every request turns a clear product into an unclear collection of options.
- **Treating limits as arrogance**: A deliberate position is useful when it helps the product serve its chosen customers.
- **Highlighting ownership without purpose**: Credit and time markers can divide content when shared work matters more.

## Worked Example
Ward Cunningham and friends stripped the original wiki of features that earlier document collaboration tools considered essential. The wiki reduced the visual emphasis on who changed a document and when. The content became ego-less and time-less, which supported a shared community. That opinionated choice helped create a key condition for Wikipedia's success. The product did not try to serve every document workflow. It committed to a different model of collaboration.

## Key Takeaways
1. State the product's approach and use it to guide feature choices.
2. Reject flexibility that weakens the product's central vision.
3. Seek customers who act as partners in that vision.
4. Use deliberate omissions to create a clear product identity.
5. Remove information that distracts from the community's main work.
