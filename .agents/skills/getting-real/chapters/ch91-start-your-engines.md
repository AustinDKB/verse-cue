# Chapter 91: Start Your Engines

## Core Idea
Getting Real starts with execution. A good idea alone does not create a successful web app. The team must combine clear writing, clean interface design, sound code, and effective promotion. Balance matters because a weak link can damage the whole product. The right people matter just as much because passionate craftspeople turn the principles into work.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Balanced Execution — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Combine the parts of product work instead of excelling in one part and failing in another.
  - **When to use**: Use it from the first idea through public delivery and promotion.
  - **How**: Make the writing match the promises. Match clean interface design with sound code. Make sure promotion helps people find the app. Find the weakest link and focus on it until it reaches the needed level.
- **Craft-Passion Team — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Build with people who care about their work as a craft.
  - **When to use**: Use it when you choose teammates or decide whom to retain.
  - **How**: Choose people who show passion, pride, care, attention to detail, and a desire to build something great. Hold onto people who will not settle for less. The team can make or break the project and company.
- **Getting Real Beyond Software — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Apply the book’s ideas to other work and life areas.
  - **When to use**: Use it when another field needs small teams, rapid action, clear limits, simple choices, or focused execution.
  - **How**: Transfer the principle, not the surface form. Look for small teams, rapid deployment, useful constraints, deliberate omissions, short time limits, or simple language in the new field.

## Key Concepts
- **Execution**: The quality of turning an idea into a delivered and known product.
- **Balance**: Sufficient strength across writing, design, code, and promotion.
- **Weak link**: The product area whose poor result limits the whole product.
- **Craft**: Serious care for the quality and details of work.
- **Passion**: Strong personal care that drives people to build something great.
- **Rapid deployment**: Fast action that helps a team accomplish work without delay.
- **Constraint**: A limit that can focus choices and produce a clear result.
- **Deliberate omission**: A product or process choice that leaves out features on purpose.
- **Simple language**: Clear language that delivers strong impact without extra complexity.

## Mental Models
- **Execution as a chain**: A promise, interface, code base, and promotion must connect. A failure in one link weakens the whole product.
- **People as the multiplier**: Mantras and methods have little value without people who can implement them well.
- **Constraints as focus**: A clear limit can remove waste and make the work stronger.
- **Translate the principle**: Search other fields for the same pattern of small scale, speed, omission, or clarity.

## Anti-patterns
- **Idea-only confidence**: Believing an idea guarantees success ignores the work required to execute it.
- **Single-strength product**: Pairing good writing with weak code, or clean design with poor promotion, leaves a weak link.
- **Weak-link neglect**: Protecting a favorite area while a poor area limits the product keeps the whole result below its potential.
- **Indifferent staffing**: People who lack care for their craft will not create the detail and quality the product needs.
- **Software-only thinking**: Treating Getting Real as a software-only method hides useful applications in other fields.

## Worked Example
The closing examples apply Getting Real outside software. Special operations forces use small teams and rapid deployment. The White Stripes use two people, streamlined songs, childlike drumming, and little studio time. Apple’s iPod gains focus by omitting an FM radio and voice recorder. A football hurry-up offense removes huddles and play-calling bureaucracy. Rachael Ray uses a 30-minute meal limit. Hemingway, Raymond Carver, and Shakespeare show how simple language or a sonnet’s fourteen-line iambic-pentameter limit can produce strong impact.

## Key Takeaways
1. Treat execution as the difference between an idea and a successful product.
2. Balance writing, interface design, code, and promotion.
3. Find the weak link and improve it before polishing stronger areas.
4. Choose passionate people who take pride in craft and details.
5. Hold onto people who will not settle for less.
6. Apply small teams, constraints, omission, speed, and clarity outside software.
