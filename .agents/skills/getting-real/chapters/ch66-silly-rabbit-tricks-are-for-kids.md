# Chapter 66: Silly Rabbit, Tricks are for Kids

## Core Idea
Avoid long-term contracts, early termination fees, and one-time setup fees. Earn recurring payment by delivering enough value each month, not by finding a tricky way to collect more cash.

A simple month-to-month relationship gives customers a clear choice. They can stay when the product helps them and cancel without a penalty when it does not.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Earn It Month to Month — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Make each billing period a new decision that the product earns.
  - **When to use**: Use it when the product charges recurring fees or when a team considers a contract to protect revenue.
  - **How**: Bill month to month. Remove the contract, setup fee, and early termination fee. Improve the product so customers choose to renew.
- **No Tricks — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Do not use hidden charges or difficult terms to increase cash.
  - **When to use**: Use it when pricing work focuses on extracting payment instead of proving value.
  - **How**: State the price plainly and let customers cancel at any time without penalty. Earn the next payment through useful service.

## Key Concepts
- **Month-to-month billing**: A recurring payment arrangement without a long commitment.
- **Long-term contract**: An agreement that requires continued payment for a fixed period.
- **Early termination fee**: A charge for ending a contract before its planned date.
- **Setup fee**: A one-time charge added before normal product use.
- **Renewal**: A customer’s choice to continue paying for another period.
- **Earned revenue**: Payment that follows continued product value rather than a trap.

## Mental Models
- **Monthly referendum**: Each billing period asks whether the product still deserves payment.
- **Value over leverage**: Improve the product instead of using contract power to protect revenue.
- **Open exit**: A customer can leave freely, so staying carries stronger evidence of product value.

## Anti-patterns
- **Contract lock-in**: A long commitment keeps a customer after the product stops earning trust.
- **Exit penalty**: An early termination fee makes departure costly instead of voluntary.
- **Setup toll**: A one-time fee adds friction before the customer sees value.
- **Cash trick**: A pricing device seeks more money without giving the customer more value.

## Worked Example
The products in the source use month-to-month billing. Customers sign no long-term contract, pay no setup fee, and can cancel at any time without a penalty. The company accepts the risk of an open exit and focuses on earning the next month’s payment through the product itself.

## Key Takeaways
1. Bill month to month when the product supports recurring use.
2. Remove long-term contracts and early termination fees.
3. Remove one-time setup fees.
4. Earn each renewal through continued value.
5. Reject pricing tricks that make exit costly.
