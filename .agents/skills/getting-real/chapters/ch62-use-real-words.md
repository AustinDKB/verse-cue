# Chapter 62: Use Real Words

## Core Idea
Replace dummy text with real and relevant words as soon as possible. Lorem ipsum turns valuable content into a visual shape and hides the conditions that customers must actually read and enter.

Real copy changes how a team sees the interface. It exposes field length, table size, form effort, and the meaning of messages. Real data also makes the team feel the customer’s path. That experience leads to a better interface than rapid entry of fake strings.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Real Words — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Use actual content instead of lorem ipsum or garbage data during design and development.
  - **When to use**: Use it as soon as a screen, form, table, or message needs content.
  - **How**: Type a real and relevant name, city, password, label, or message. Repeat values when the customer must repeat them.
- **Customer’s Long Road — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Follow the same data-entry path that customers follow.
  - **When to use**: Use it when a team wants to rush through forms with meaningless values.
  - **How**: Enter realistic values at normal steps and notice the time, field length, errors, and effort. Design from that experience.
- **Content Is Design Input — Tom Smith, designer and developer, from “I Hate Lorem Ipsum and Lorem Ipsum Users”**: Treat text as information that shapes understanding and reveals design opportunities.
  - **When to use**: Use it when a team treats copy as decoration that can wait until the end.
  - **How**: Put meaningful content in the interface. Check whether the words fit, remain readable, explain the task, and suggest useful improvements.

## Key Concepts
- **Real copy**: Actual words that a customer can read and understand.
- **Lorem ipsum**: Dummy text that imitates the shape of content without its meaning.
- **Garbage data**: Fake values entered only to move quickly through a form.
- **Field length**: The amount of space that real values need in an input.
- **Table expansion**: Growth or contraction that real content causes in a table.
- **Data input**: Information that a person enters into the application.
- **Content meaning**: The information that text gives to a person, beyond its visual shape.
- **Interface reality**: The experience that appears when real people enter and read real information.

## Mental Models
- **Text as structure**: Think of copy as a load-bearing part of the interface, not filler around visual elements.
- **Reality veil**: Dummy text hides the interface conditions that real content exposes.
- **Customer mirror**: Enter data as the customer does so the team can feel the actual effort.
- **Content opportunity**: Real words can reveal a missing instruction, a better label, or a new design need.

## Anti-patterns
- **Lorem ipsum veil**: Dummy text hides field length, table behavior, meaning, and readability problems.
- **Garbage sprint**: Fake strings let the team move fast while avoiding the customer’s actual path.
- **Paste shortcut**: Pasted content removes the physical effort and sequence that a customer experiences.
- **Visual-only design**: Treating text as shape can make it too small, too vague, or hard to understand.
- **Late content**: Waiting until the end makes real copy a surprise instead of a design input.

## Worked Example
A team builds a form that asks for a name, city, password, and repeated password. Instead of using strings such as random letters and numbers, each person types a real name and city and enters the password twice. The team then sees the true field lengths and the effort of the form. It also fills a table with relevant content to see whether the table expands or contracts. The team can now change the interface before customers meet those conditions.

## Key Takeaways
1. Replace lorem ipsum with real, relevant words early.
2. Type realistic values instead of using garbage data.
3. Repeat values when the customer must repeat them.
4. Use real content to test field lengths and table behavior.
5. Follow the customer’s data-entry path to understand effort.
6. Treat content meaning as part of interface design.
