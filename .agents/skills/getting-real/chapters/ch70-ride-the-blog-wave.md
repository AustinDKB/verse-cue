# Chapter 70: Ride the Blog Wave

## Core Idea
Blogging can promote an application more effectively than advertising, and it costs much less. A useful blog earns readers before it asks them to buy. Those readers can carry the product message to other people through links, posts, and recommendations.

The method works because the blog provides helpful advice, tips, tricks, and links instead of acting as a product notice board. A team with limited time or money can build attention through useful material and then use that existing attention when the product launches.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Promote-via-Blog — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Use a useful blog as the main path for product promotion instead of starting with traditional advertising.
  - **When to use**: Use it when advertising costs too much or when the team cannot measure advertising value without spending even more.
  - **How**: Publish helpful advice, tips, tricks, links, and interesting observations. Build a regular readership. Tell readers about the product when it solves a related problem. Make it easy for other writers and blogs to spread the news.
- **Audience Before Promotion — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Earn attention with useful material before asking people to notice a product.
  - **When to use**: Use it before a launch and during the product’s ongoing life.
  - **How**: Make the blog valuable on its own. Let product news enter an existing relationship with readers instead of forcing every post to sell.
- **Blog Wave — Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson, and Matthew Linderman**: Let one useful announcement travel through connected readers and public blogs.
  - **When to use**: Use it when a product has a clear story that readers can repeat.
  - **How**: Publish the announcement to a trusted audience. Give interested writers enough value to mention it. Follow the spread and keep publishing useful material.

## Key Concepts
- **Advertising**: Paid promotion whose value can cost more to measure than to buy.
- **Blog**: A public source of useful, regular written material.
- **Helpful advice**: Information that gives readers value without requiring a purchase.
- **Unique reader**: One distinct person who visits a blog during a period.
- **Signal vs. Noise**: The authors’ blog that built a large audience through useful posts and anecdotes.
- **Visibility**: The amount of public attention a product receives.
- **Blog-based marketing**: Promotion that travels through useful blog content and reader links.
- **Launch post**: A public announcement that introduces a product to an existing audience.

## Mental Models
- **Value before ask**: Give readers a reason to return before asking them to consider the product.
- **Borrowed distribution**: A trusted blog audience can carry a product farther than a small team can carry it alone.
- **Compounding mention**: One useful post can create links, comments, and later references that continue after publication.
- **Low-cost experiment**: A blog lets a team test messages and interest without buying a large advertising campaign.

## Anti-patterns
- **Ad-first promotion**: Buying attention before creating useful material spends money before trust exists.
- **Product-only blog**: A blog that only announces features gives readers no reason to return between launches.
- **Single-post silence**: One announcement without continued useful writing lets attention disappear.
- **Measurement trap**: Spending more to evaluate an advertisement than the advertisement costs makes the channel harder to justify.
- **Closed distribution**: Keeping launch news inside the company prevents readers and other blogs from extending its reach.

## Worked Example
The authors used their Signal vs. Noise blog, which reached thousands of unique readers each week, to introduce Basecamp. A single post about Ta-da Lists led to mentions on more than 200 blogs and more than 12,000 signups within a few weeks. Backpack spread even faster, with more than 10,000 people signing up within 24 hours of launch. The examples show the sequence: build a useful readership, publish product news there, and let connected writers extend the announcement.

## Key Takeaways
1. Publish useful advice before you need launch attention.
2. Use blogging when advertising or advertising measurement costs too much.
3. Keep the blog valuable even when no product announcement exists.
4. Launch to an audience that already trusts the source.
5. Make useful product news easy for other blogs to repeat.
6. Keep publishing after the first wave of attention.
